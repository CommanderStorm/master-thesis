\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Vector map styles are developed separately from the tilesets they reference.
As a result, tiles carry data that a given style never reads, and styles carry logic that a renderer re-evaluates on every frame even though it could be resolved ahead of time.
We formalized the joint optimization of style and tile as a rewrite system that preserves visual equivalence and is idempotent, and implemented it in three levels.
Expression rules simplify the style, a second level reorganizes the layer graph, and data passes remove unreferenced tile content and re-encode the rest as \ac{MLT}.

On the Germany \ac{OMT} tileset, \ac{MLT} encoding alone makes tiles \qty{12}{\percent} smaller than gzip-compressed \ac{MVT}, and style-driven shaving removes a median of \qty{29}{\percent} of bytes per style.
Together, the pipeline improves load times by a median of \qty{71}{\percent}, with no soundness regressions across the 10-style evaluation corpus and the 12-style generalization corpus.
The gains of the three levels are roughly additive, so each can be adopted independently of the others.

Future work falls into three directions.
The first is moving the pipeline out of its offline batch stage.
The style optimizer depends only on the style and could run at deployment time, tile shaving could run as a serving-time filter \citep{deshpandeAdaptiveQueryProcessing2006}, and re-encoding could move to the edge or into a decoder shipped with the tiles \citep{gienieczkoAnyBloxFrameworkSelfDecoding2025}.
The second is the rewrite engine, which applies rules in a fixed order until a fixpoint.
Equality saturation \citep{tateEqualitySaturationNew2009,willseyEggFastExtensible2021} would remove this ordering dependence.
The third is coverage, since symbol-layer merging and the sensitivity of our thresholds to other tilesets remain open.
