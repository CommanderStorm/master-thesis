\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Web maps rendered from vector tiles divide their work across two timelines.
When a tile arrives, the client decodes it and binds the active style to every feature it holds.
On each rendered frame, the client re-evaluates the view-dependent parts of that style and issues draw calls to the GPU.
Network and decode cost bound the first timeline, and the graphics device bounds the second.
MapLibre GL~JS and Mapbox GL~JS both follow this model, streaming tiled geometry into the browser and driving a WebGL or WebGPU pipeline from a declarative style document.
While both timelines stay inside budget the map pans and zooms smoothly, and once either exceeds it the interaction stutters.

The penalty grows with reach.
A tileset served to millions of sessions pays for every redundant byte many times over.
Oversized tiles lengthen transfer, layers that never draw waste frame time, and attributes no client reads inflate decode cost.
On phones the same waste surfaces as heat and battery drain, and over the intermittent links found on trains and aircraft it surfaces as visible stalls.

Map production keeps styling and tiling apart.
Cartographers tune a style for appearance against a tileset that was encoded for general use, and no stage inspects the two together.
That separation leaves an opening.
Suppose a filter tests a feature property and constant folding against per-tile statistics collapses that filter into a form that no longer names the property.
An encoder aware of this could drop the property's column from every tile, a reduction that neither style rewriting nor blind data pruning reaches on its own.
Whether such couplings deliver gains beyond the sum of their parts is the question this paper sets out to answer.

We study this style- and data-driven co-optimization and make the following contributions.

\begin{itemize}
    \item A \textbf{formalization of joint style and tile co-optimization} as a transformation $\mathcal{O}: \mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{T} \to \mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{T}$ held to visual equivalence and strict idempotency.
    Compiler optimization and columnar encoding each cover one half of this space, and we are aware of no prior treatment that couples both under a single soundness criterion.
    \item A \textbf{three-level optimizer}.
    Expression-level peephole rewrites run to a fixpoint.
    Structural passes then rewrite a typed style model through dead layer elimination, metadata refinement, and layer merging.
    A tile level closes the pipeline with \emph{style-driven tile shaving}, which removes unused properties, values, geometry types, and feature ids, followed by \emph{adaptive multi-strategy encoding} into the \ac{MLT} columnar format.
    \item An \textbf{implementation} that connects the MapLibre style runtime to the \ac{MLT} tile pipeline end to end.
    \item An \textbf{evaluation} over multiple style corpora and 18 scenarios with a cumulative ablation, reporting load-time, tile-volume, and frame-rate effects and settling the additive-versus-synergistic question at both aggregate and per-scenario granularity.
\end{itemize}
