\begin{abstract}
Interactive web maps built on vector tiles face tight performance budgets: every frame fetches tiles over the network, decodes them, evaluates style expressions per feature, and submits draw calls.
Yet style authoring and tile data preparation have traditionally been treated as independent concerns.
We investigate joint, automatic style and data optimization for MapLibre rendering pipelines, asking whether style-level and data-level transformations compose additively or synergistically.
We design and implement a standalone optimizer operating at three levels: peephole expression rewrites over style JSON via fixpoint iteration, structural passes over a typed style document, and a data-level pruning advisory that strips unused properties, geometry types, and feature values before re-encoding tiles into the columnar MapLibre Tiles (MLT) format.
Across a deep corpus of styles and scenarios, tile shaving dominated the load-time improvements; composed end-to-end, the full pipeline reduced load time by a median of 71\% and increased frame rate by a median of 152\%.
Our MLT encoder cut total tile volume by 28\% over the uncompressed reference and 12\% over gzip-compressed Mapbox Vector Tiles.
Style-level passes yielded a median 31.4\% raw size reduction on unseen styles with visually equivalent output.
In aggregate the two levels compose additively rather than synergistically, and on data volume this holds for every style.
Per-scenario load-time synergy is common but small and style-dependent, averaging out without lifting overall gains.
\end{abstract}
